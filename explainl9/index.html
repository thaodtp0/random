Reading Passage 1 · Questions 1–13

Question 1

B
📍 Find it in: B

Evidence: dance might hold some of the answers; not ... everyone should dance their way to work

Why: The writer considers dance as a design tool, not literally making commuters dance.

Watch out: Do not mistake a rejected illustration for the actual proposal.

Question 2

C
📍 Find it in: C

Evidence: medieval builders ... intimate knowledge ... ; the design practices created by these new technologies ... far managing the technical complexity

Why: Contrasts older experiential building with modern technology-led design.

Watch out: Both time periods must appear in the same paragraph.

Question 3

F
📍 Find it in: F

Evidence: shares with engineering the aim of designing patterns of movement within limitations of space

Why: Explicitly identifies a shared goal.

Watch out: Look for shared purpose, not just two topic words.

Question 4

D
📍 Find it in: D

Evidence: failed to take into account that purpose-built street cafes could not operate in the hot sun without the protective awnings

Why: The unexpected heat/sun problem follows from ignoring local climate.

Watch out: The passage does not say the plan itself was too expensive.

Question 5

E
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: These barriers ... divide communities and decrease opportunities for healthy transport. As a result, many are now being removed

Why: The original safety measures caused unintended harms, so they are being removed.

Watch out: Identify both original intention and reason for reversal.

Question 6

A
📍 Find it in: A

Evidence: The ways we travel affect our physical and mental health, our social lives, our access to work and culture

Why: Lists broad effects of travel on everyday life.

Watch out: Do not select a paragraph only describing a road design.

Question 7

safety
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: an engineering solution to pedestrian safety

Why: The summary asks what guard rails were intended to improve.

Watch out: ONE WORD only: safety.

Question 8

traffic
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: prioritise the smooth flow of traffic

Why: Traffic is what engineers sought to keep moving.

Watch out: Do not write flow: the summary requires the thing moving.

Question 9

carriageway
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: divide the crossing into two — one for each carriageway

Why: The exact singular noun follows “each”.

Watch out: Use singular carriageway, not carriageways.

Question 10

mobile
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: those that are the least mobile

Why: Least mobile people face the greatest psychological barriers.

Watch out: Least = less in the paraphrase; answer is mobile.

Question 11

dangerous
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: encouraging others to make dangerous crossings

Why: People may cross in a riskier manner to avoid rails.

Watch out: Change adjective + noun into adjective + way.

Question 12

communities
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: they divide communities

Why: Rails separate communities rather than physically splitting the road only.

Watch out: Avoid choosing “crossings”: the text states communities.

Question 13

healthy
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: decrease opportunities for healthy transport

Why: Rails can discourage healthy modes of travel.

Watch out: Do not add a second word; one word required.

Reading Passage 2 · Questions 14–26

Question 14

F
📍 Find it in: F

Evidence: technology ... could prevent mass extinctions in the future

Why: Shapiro discusses preventing the disappearance of many species.

Watch out: “Prevent” indicates future avoidance.

Question 15

A
📍 Find it in: A

Evidence: using cloning technology to turn the DNA of extinct animals into a fertilised embryo

Why: Explains how DNA is used in a proposed revival process.

Watch out: Focus on the mechanism, not the animal example.

Question 16

D
📍 Find it in: D

Evidence: re-establish that forest disturbance, thereby creating a habitat necessary for a great many other native species to thrive

Why: The lost pigeon’s nesting behaviour could restore forest habitat.

Watch out: The question asks about the habitat, not just the extinct animal.

Question 17

A
📍 Find it in: A

Evidence: the last living specimen died at Cincinnati Zoo ... 1 September 1914

Why: Provides the exact date of the passenger pigeon’s extinction.

Watch out: A date is stronger evidence than a general historical reference.

Question 18

genetic traits
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: By pinpointing which genetic traits made it possible for mammoths to survive the icy climate

Why: Direct match to the survival characteristics the team wants to identify.

Watch out: Two words maximum.

Question 19

heat loss
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: extra insulating fat, all for the purpose of reducing heat loss in the tundra

Why: Physical adaptations minimise heat loss.

Watch out: Two words, not “temperature”.

Question 20

ears
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: smaller ears, thicker hair, and extra insulating fat

Why: The summary already includes thicker hair; ears must be smaller.

Watch out: Write ears, not smaller ears (the grammar supplies “reduced size”).

Question 21

(insulating) fat
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: extra insulating fat

Why: Fat is the additional feature after smaller ears and thicker hair.

Watch out: Both “fat” and “insulating fat” accepted; the summary already says “more”.

Question 22

(carbon) emissions
📍 Find it in: E

Evidence: reducing carbon emissions ... mitigate emissions from melting permafrost

Why: The claimed environmental benefit concerns emissions.

Watch out: Both “emissions” and “carbon emissions” accepted.

Question 23

B
📍 Find it in: B

Evidence: Tasmanian devils ... help to ensure that devils are never again subjected to risks of this kind

Why: Archer proposes returning thylacines to address a threat to Tasmanian devils.

Watch out: Do not confuse Archer with Church’s environmental claims.

Question 24

C
📍 Find it in: F

Evidence: focus the debate on how ... technology could be used to fully understand why various species went extinct

Why: Shapiro prioritises understanding extinction causes.

Watch out: “Why” signals causes.

Question 25

A
📍 Find it in: D

Evidence: creating a habitat necessary for a great many other native species to thrive

Why: Novak describes ecosystem-wide benefits.

Watch out: Not every benefit of revival is about climate.

Question 26

C
📍 Find it in: F

Evidence: what we are doing today is not enough

Why: Shapiro says existing conservation efforts are insufficient.

Watch out: Strong direct paraphrase.

Reading Passage 3 · Questions 27–40

Question 27

C
📍 Find it in: opening paragraph

Evidence: rich resources for studying human psychology, ranging from the development of language to the neuroscience of social perception

Why: The author stresses laughter as a subject of scientific inquiry.

Watch out: The paragraph does not argue laughter is universal among animals.

Question 28

A
📍 Find it in: paragraph 2

Evidence: live audiences ... laugh tracks ... help people at home feel like they were in a social situation

Why: Douglass used recorded laughter to recreate the feeling of a group.

Watch out: A is supported; the passage does not say viewers needed instructions.

Question 29

B
📍 Find it in: paragraph 3

Evidence: remarkably consistent: worldwide, people’s guesses were correct approximately 60% of the time

Why: Cross-cultural consistency is the noteworthy result.

Watch out: The 24 societies are a means, not the main finding.

Question 30

B
📍 Find it in: paragraph 4

Evidence: the two high-status members ... would swap roles ... with the low-status members

Why: Roles were exchanged during the experiment.

Watch out: Do not infer friendship or distress.

Question 31

D
📍 Find it in: paragraph 5

Evidence: newcomers produced more dominant laughter ... low-status individuals may achieve higher status ... by the way they laugh

Why: Laughter may signal a temporary shift in social status.

Watch out: D matches the key; A/B/C are too absolute or contradict findings.

Question 32

F
📍 Find it in: paragraph 6

Evidence: others a relaxing scene with dolphins swimming in the ocean

Why: One of the three videos was relaxing.

Watch out: Option F is relaxing; avoid confusing with “amusing”.

Question 33

H
📍 Find it in: paragraph 7

Evidence: Students who had watched the Mr. Bean video ended up spending significantly more time working on the task

Why: The humorous video led to longer persistence.

Watch out: The option H “amusing” corresponds to Mr. Bean.

Question 34

C
📍 Find it in: paragraph 6

Evidence: a tedious task ... had to cross out every instance of the letter e over two pages of text

Why: The assigned activity was deliberately boring.

Watch out: C “boring” paraphrases tedious.

Question 35

D
📍 Find it in: final paragraph

Evidence: humour has been found to help relieve stress

Why: Relieving stress means reducing anxiety.

Watch out: D anxiety, not “emotion”.

Question 36

E
📍 Find it in: final paragraph

Evidence: humour is not only enjoyable but more importantly, energising

Why: The physical/mental effect is energising or stimulating.

Watch out: E stimulating, not G enjoyment.

Question 37

NOT GIVEN
📍 Find it in: paragraph 3

Evidence: whether they thought the people laughing were friends or strangers ... correct approximately 60% of the time

Why: The study gives an overall accuracy, not separate accuracy for friends versus strangers.

Watch out: NOT GIVEN: no comparison of the two categories is reported.

Question 38

YES
📍 Find it in: paragraph 5

Evidence: high-status individuals were rated as high-status whether they produced their natural dominant laugh or tried to do a submissive one

Why: Predictions about identifying high-status people were supported.

Watch out: YES is about the high-status group, not every participant.

Question 39

NO
📍 Find it in: paragraph 7

Evidence: Participants were allowed to quit the task at any point

Why: There was no fixed completion time.

Watch out: NO: the statement contradicts the described procedure.

Question 40

NO
📍 Find it in: final paragraph

Evidence: traditional view ... implies that individuals should avoid things such as humour ... [researchers] suggest that humour is ... energising

Why: The authors challenge, rather than support, the traditional view.

Watch out: NO: “in line with” means agrees with, but the passage disagrees.
